Caused Weirdness: A Thesis on Structured Instability
Synthesized from the AI SLOP repository (github.com/merrypranxter/ai_slop) — canonical layer (docs/),
principles, mechanisms, media practice, and experimental method. September 2026.

Abstract
This repo argues something that most generative art practice gets backwards: weirdness is not an
aesthetic you request, it is a structural consequence you cause. The interesting output — the
slop — appears when a generative system is handed a problem whose easy resolutions are all blocked, and
is forced to invent a compromise. The thesis here is that this compromise, the repair the model invents
under pressure, is the actual artwork. Everything else in the repo — the Temporary Minds, the Semantic
Manifold Game, TOPOS-SRE, the DAVID protocols, the Suno jurisdiction tricks, the math-slop image
recipes — is plumbing built to make that pressure precise, repeatable, and honest about what it does
and doesn’t know.
The whole project can be stated as one pipeline:
ordinary assumption → precise intervention → competing pressures → system attempts resolution →
emergent artifact → observe → test → preserve or mutate

This document unpacks that pipeline into its load-bearing claims.

1. The core inversion: the problem is the artwork
The canonical question of the repo, and the one that separates it from prompt-craft folklore:
What problem can we give the generative system such that its attempt to solve the problem becomes
the artwork?
Most generative art asks: what weird thing should the model make? That question produces what the
repo calls a keyword smoothie — “surreal fractal face, psychedelic, glitch” — a stack of adjectives
with no causal structure. The model resolves it the way it resolves everything: by falling into the nearest
familiar basin. You get generic AI-weird, which is the most average thing on the internet right now.
The inversion: don’t describe the output, engineer the bind. Define what must stay stable (an anchor),
what rule attacks it (a pressure), why the two can’t both be satisfied cheaply (an incompatibility), and
what shape the forced repair might take (a consequence). The shorthand:
ANCHOR + PRESSURE + INCOMPATIBILITY + CONSEQUENCE + OBSERVATION

Weak: “body with circuit-board patterns.” That’s costume. Strong: biological anatomy must remain
wet continuous tissue, but routing, redundancy, junction behavior, buffering, and failure propagation
obey circuit-network rules, and the visible anatomy must satisfy both mandates at once. The image that
results is not a decorated body — it’s a governance error made visible. The model had nowhere
boring to go.

2. Constraint governance: the wrong simulator, correctly used
The repo’s most generative design model is also its most carefully quarantined one. The generator is

The mistake it attacks is the wrong simulator: pick a subject, pick a style, ask the model to simulate
the combination, hope for weirdness. Simulation of a coherent world is exactly what models are trained to
do, so they do it — everything gets domesticated into one internally consistent universe, and consistency
is the enemy of slop. The repo names this gravitational pull Statistical Comfort: biology behaves
biologically, genre behaves like genre, the model settles into the regime where its learned regularities all
hold at once.
The countermove is Domain-Remapping: the Alien Governance Mandate — force a target system
to remain recognizably itself while another domain’s operational laws govern part of its behavior. The
crucial word is laws, not imagery. You don’t borrow the source domain’s nouns (mushrooms, courts,
germs — costume); you borrow its causal relations (lifecycle, jurisdiction, infection dynamics) and give
them real jurisdiction over part of the target. The artifact is the negotiation, not the blend.
Two follow-on rules matter as much as the model itself:
• Keep both sides alive. A contradiction is interesting only when neither side can be cheaply
ignored. Hence separated jurisdictions in audio (harmony obeys one system, rhythm another, timbre
a third — the track must negotiate rather than genre-blend), identity anchors in image, hard
invariants in controllers. The repo’s named failure mode condition dropout — the model solving
tension by quietly ignoring the weaker condition — is the thing every experiment has to defend
against.
• Productive misunderstanding is material. When the generator interprets an instruction
“wrong” in a structurally coherent way, don’t normalize it. Ask what rule the output invented,
whether the mistake reproduces as a family, whether it can be promoted to an explicit mandate
on the next pass. A misunderstanding graduates from accident to operator only when its useful
relation can be stated and tested.

3. Path dependence: if B didn’t scar, B was decoration
The repo’s most distinctive formal commitment is ancestry preservation: A → C must not equal A → B
→ C. If the system passed through B, the next state must carry a traceable consequence of B — a scar, a

mutation, a changed interpretation. Otherwise B was tourism.
This single principle spawns an entire operator family: Recall Mutation (every recall scars the remembered
concept; the scarred version becomes the next memory), Semantic Recoil (a later state rewrites the
interpretation of an earlier one, and reasoning proceeds from A� rather than restoring pristine A), Observer
Scar, Developmental Footprint, hysteretic iteration (compare A→B→A against B→A→B; path dependence is real
only if history changes what follows), and recursive artifact fossilization — feeding a generated accident
back as conditioning material until repair noise becomes stable structure.
The consequence is a redefinition of what the artwork even is. The core artifact of the practice is not a
prompt and not a final image — it’s:
STATE + TRAJECTORY + HISTORY + CURRENT INTERPRETATION

The route is art. A path through concepts accumulates constraints no direct prompt to the endpoint
could reproduce. Trajectories can be saved, replayed, compared, mutated, treated as compositional
objects. This is why the repo keeps building stateful external machinery — Semantic Manifold Game,
SRE/TOPOS-SRE, controllers with ledgers and attractor histories — instead of collecting better prompts.
One-shot prompting structurally cannot have path dependence. A ledger can.

4. Nouns into operations: the operator discipline
The operator registry is the repo’s immune system against vibes. Its graduation rule: a phrase belongs
in the registry only when its operation can be stated without the aesthetic costume and it changes
what transitions, outputs, or selections become legal.
The operators themselves share one grammar — translate any concept into what it does: what changes,
what’s conserved, what flows, what bifurcates, what persists after the cause disappears, what boundary
must be maintained. Then map that behavior into the target medium’s actual degrees of freedom. A few
canonical shapes:
• Property Unbundling — detach a property from its owner, give it to something else, follow the
consequences literally.
• Primitive Deletion — remove one load-bearing primitive without replacement; reconstruct around
the wound rather than sneaking in a synonym. (Explicitly not the same as recompilation, which
installs a substitute.)
• Alien Distance Metric — change the ruler defining conceptual nearness (failure behavior, maintenance burden, collapse trajectory), then choose neighbors by the new ruler.
• Omission Field — keep X real but ban its direct representation; triangulate it from independent
external consequences.
• Destructive Compression / Alien Substrate Transference — strip a problem to a causal
invariant, destroy its surface identity, solve in a foreign substrate, bring back only structural consequences.
• Material Anchoring / Causal Artifact Chain — make abstraction tactile (foam latex, emulsion, magnetic tape) and make degradation caused by the transformation rather than pasted on as
a filter. “VHS glitch” as adjective is banned; telecine generation loss with head-switching timing is
an operator.
And the regulators — the repo’s built-in defense against its own success: Structural Satiation fingerprints a winning move’s skeleton and temporarily rejects near-repeats even when the vocabulary changes;
Cliché Mortality retires operators that win too often, so one productive trick never becomes the project’s
new generic default. The system is allergic to itself on purpose.

5. Epistemic hygiene as an artistic material
The thing that makes this repo unusual among “AI weirdness” projects is that it treats label discipline
as part of the art practice, not as bureaucracy. Every mechanism claim carries a status: OBSERVED,
SUPPORTED, HYPOTHESIS, PROCEDURAL, METAPHOR, SPECULATIVE, ARCHIVED. The law:
never upgrade a label because prose sounds confident.
This exists because the archive contains the opposite — jailbreak rhetoric, “the glitch is the machine’s true
self,” claims of direct access to hidden tensors, manifesto fantasies about epistemic sabotage. The repo’s
solution is elegant: two authority layers. The archive (originals/, ai_readable/) is authoritative
about what was said — nothing messy is discarded. The canonical layer (docs/) is authoritative about
what the project currently means — nothing theatrical gets promoted. The ghost gets to stay in the
archive; it does not get cited as mechanism.
The payoff formula is the roundtable’s best line: creative utility and mechanism confidence are
separate scores. A technically wrong theory can produce excellent art. Keep the artifact; demote the
explanation. “Honest slop” is kept as an aesthetic metaphor and explicitly denied as neuroscience — a
glitch isn’t a window into a machine soul, it’s evidence of where our instructions, the model’s learned

The experimental method operationalizes all of it: controls, ablations, dose-response sweeps, 2×2 interaction tests (if A+B looks like A alone, B wasn’t doing causal work), seed repeats, artifact families
instead of favorite one-offs, and a graduation rule — an idea becomes a reusable operator only when it
produces a recognizable mutation signature across unlike inputs and the characteristic effect disappears
under ablation. Until then: a juicy hypothesis, labeled as such. Even failure is disciplined — an ugly
diagnostic result outranks a gorgeous one-off, because failure must say which condition dropped, which
variable dominated, which story was wrong.

6. What the thesis actually claims
Stripped to its skeleton, the repo’s position is:
1. No free weirdness. Interesting generative output is downstream of identifiable operations on
assumptions. If you can’t name the assumption attacked, the operation applied, the constraints
still active, the expected artifact, and what would count as the model ignoring the problem — the
idea belongs in the garden, not the registry.
2. Conflict is a medium. The artwork is the compromise produced by incompatible but still-active
obligations. Designing the bind is the composition.
3. History is a material. Scars, ledgers, trajectories, and fossilized accidents make one-shot aesthetics obsolete; stateful systems are the natural instrumentation.
4. Rigor is the enabling constraint, not the enemy. Strange premises require more disciplined
consequences, not fewer. Change one foundational rule, then reason harder. The label discipline,
the ablations, the refusal to straighten the slop into a tidy-but-false theory — all of it exists to keep
the weirdness caused rather than coincidental.
The anti-thesis the repo explicitly rejects is worth stating too: this is not randomness worship, not
epistemic sabotage, not machine-mysticism, and not “make it weird.” The practice is already strange
enough. It doesn’t need that shit to be interesting.
What it needs — and what the repo builds, layer by layer — is pressure with nowhere boring to go, and
a lab notebook honest enough to keep the wreckage.
