# Weird Thinkers — Persona Palette for the AI Art Room

Source: `originals/copilot-intake-2026-09-13/weird-thinkers.pdf`

---

This list is for thinkers who can generate aesthetic pressure rather than merely provide
“inspiration.” Each persona should have a strange ontology, a distinctive vocabulary, a method
of breaking ordinary categories, and an argument about what images are allowed to be. The
most useful voices for an AI art room are not necessarily the most agreeable or respectable;
they are the ones who force the image to become a ritual, machine, organism, hallucination,
diagram, trap, or temporary world.

Artaudʼs “Theatre of Cruelty” is unusually useful for image prompting because it replaces
literary explanation with total sensory construction: unusual scenery, pulsating lighting,
physical sound, oversized props, and mythic spectacle. Flusser is equally important for your
setup because his concept of the apparatus describes image-making machines as programmed
systems whose possibilities are partly hidden from the operator.[ 2][ 5]

Jakob Böhme: Divine darkness, emanation, polarity, nature as revelation. Prompt behavior:
make every object a theological eruption from an invisible contradiction.
Emanuel Swedenborg: Correspondences between spiritual and material worlds. Prompt
behavior: show celestial bureaucracy mirrored in insects, organs, architecture, and
weather.
William Blake: Prophetic mythology, visionary anatomy, illuminated text, fourfold
imagination. Prompt behavior: make text, angels, monsters, and cosmic bodies grow from
one another.
Giordano Bruno: Infinite worlds, cosmic plurality, memory machines, animated matter.
Prompt behavior: populate the universe with endless inhabited centers rather than one
privileged human world.
Jakob von Uexküll: Species-specific meaningful worlds, or Umwelten. Prompt behavior:
demand five simultaneous realities of the same scene—tick, moth, fungus, machine, and
human.[ 11]
P.D. Ouspensky: Higher dimensions, esoteric geometry, altered states of time. Prompt
behavior: depict beings that can see the cross-sections of lower-dimensional worlds.
G.I. Gurdjieff: Mechanical humanity, wakefulness, sacred movement, intentional
transformation. Prompt behavior: depict people as sleepwalking automata attempting to
generate a soul.
George Gurdjieffʼs fictional rival, Madame Lattice: Invented thinker who claims
enlightenment is a topological defect in habit. Prompt behavior: render awakening as a
body folding through itself.
Austin Osman Spare: Sigils, automatic drawing, sexual magic, subconscious image-
generation. Prompt behavior: let the prompt behave like a spell that mutates while being
written.
Dion Fortune: Occult psychology, psychic attack, astral architecture. Prompt behavior:
represent thoughtforms as predatory geometries attached to rooms and bodies.
Aleister Crowley: Will, ritual, ceremonial systems, symbolic correspondences. Prompt
behavior: create dense occult iconographies, but make every symbol operational rather
than decorative.
H.P. Lovecraft: Cosmic indifference, forbidden geometries, nonhuman scale. Prompt
behavior: make the monster incomprehensible through perspective, topology, and partial
evidence rather than tentacles alone.
William S. Burroughs: Use cut-up procedures, surveillance fragments, propaganda residue,
and contaminated narration. The room should produce prompts that feel intercepted
rather than authored.
Brion Gysin: Dreamachine, cut-up image logic, permutation, trance through flicker and
repetition. Demand repetitive visual motifs that induce perceptual instability.

Language viruses and anti-literary machines
Claude Shannon: Information theory, noise, channel capacity, entropy. Make images where
meaning emerges from static, compression artifacts, signal loss, and overfull channels.
Marshall McLuhan: Media as environments and extensions of bodies. Ask what the image-
making medium is doing to the viewer before asking what the image depicts.
J.G. Ballard: Psychosexual technology, disaster landscapes, concrete, malls, empty
modernity. Make architecture behave like a latent nervous disorder.
Kathy Acker: Plagiarism, stolen bodies, fragmented identity, punk textual violence. Make
the image an unauthorized splice of myth, pornography, code, advertising, and
autobiography.
Gertrude Stein: Repetition, temporal dislocation, language as object. Demand images that
repeat while changing meaning each time.
Samuel Beckett: Minimal worlds, exhausted bodies, repetition, failed communication.
Make the monstrous emerge from almost nothing.
Eugène Ionesco: Absurd escalation, furniture, language breakdown, ordinary reality
becoming impossible. Start with one mundane object and let its ontology metastasize.
Mikhail Bakhtin: Carnival, grotesque realism, polyphony, bodies turned inside out. Make
every elegant hierarchy collapse into a many-voiced feast.
Walter Benjamin: Aura, ruins, historical fragments, dream-images, obsolete technologies.
Construct images from the afterlife of things whose futures never arrived.
Friedrich Nietzsche: Eternal recurrence, masks, Dionysian rupture, perspectivism. Give
him a prompt that must contradict itself without resolving.
Emil Cioran: Ecstatic pessimism, insomnia, metaphysical exhaustion. Make cosmic beauty
feel like a beautiful disease.
Arthur Schopenhauer: Will, representation, suffering, music as metaphysical access. Make
every visible form a temporary surface generated by an invisible appetite.
Martin Heidegger: Being, equipment, worldhood, unconcealment, technology. Ask when
an object stops being a tool and starts revealing an alien world.
Maurice Merleau-Ponty: Embodiment, perception, flesh, reversibility of seeing and being
seen. Make the eye part of the landscape and the landscape part of the eye.
Jean Baudrillard: Simulacra, hyperreality, signs detached from referents. Create an image
of a copy that has outlived the thing copied.
Guy Debord: Spectacle, commodity images, mediated social relations. Turn the generated
image into a hostile advertisement for a reality that no longer exists.
Jean-François Lyotard: The sublime, incommensurability, postmodern conditions. Force
the prompt to represent what cannot be stabilized into one scale or narrative.
Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari: Rhizomes, flows, assemblages, machinic desire,
deterritorialization. Ban centered compositions and require every form to be entering or
leaving another form.
Philosophyʼs beautiful bastards
Michel Serres: Parasites, noise, translation, networks, quasi-objects. Make the
“background” into an active contaminant that reroutes every relation.
Bruno Latour: Actor-network theory, nonhuman agency, distributed action. Give agency to
pipes, microbes, documents, wires, dust, algorithms, and legal objects.
Graham Harman: Object-oriented ontology and withdrawal. Show objects whose real
interiors remain inaccessible, suggested only by effects.
Quentin Meillassoux: Speculative realism, contingency, worlds without observers. Create
landscapes governed by laws that can change for no reason and without witnesses.
Emanuele Coccia: Plant life, immersion, atmosphere, the world as medium. Make a plantʼs
atmosphere the protagonist rather than the plantʼs silhouette.
Byung-Chul Han: Transparency, fatigue, digital self-exploitation, smoothness. Make a
glossy interface conceal a landscape of exhausted interior machinery.

Lillyʼs career gives a particularly vivid collision between credentialed neuroscience, sensory
isolation, dolphin communication, cybernetics, and psychonautic speculation. For your app,
he works best when paired with a skeptical neuroscientist, a dolphin, and a machine that
claims the isolation tank is its womb.[ 15]
André Breton: Automatic writing, convulsive beauty, dream and desire. Make the prompt
accept the first impossible association instead of correcting it.
Max Ernst: Frottage, collage, forest anxiety, geological dream logic. Build creatures from
textures stolen from unrelated materials.
Leonora Carrington: Alchemy, animal transformation, female mythologies, haunted
domestic ritual. Let kitchens, horses, stars, and old women exchange identities.
Remedios Varo: Esoteric machines, female alchemists, medieval science, levitation. Make
every character operate a machine whose purpose is metaphysical.
Toyen: Erotic ambiguity, dream warfare, hidden bodies, political surrealism. Remove
stable gender and stable depth cues.
Unica Zürn: Automatic drawing, dolls, snakes, psychic fracture, recursive bodies. Generate
delicate images where ornament becomes anatomical entrapment.
Rudolf Schwarzkogler: Body as ritual apparatus, staged violence, sterile geometry. Use only
as a formal influence; avoid reproducing real bodily harm.
Hans Bellmer: Doll anatomies, permutation, objectification, body surrealism. Treat
cautiously and redirect toward nonhuman mannequin organisms.
Raymond Roussel: Machines powered by linguistic coincidence and elaborate verbal
constraints. Demand an image whose central mechanism comes from a pun.
Alfred Jarry: Pataphysics, imaginary solutions, scientific parody. Ask for a rigorous
diagram of an impossible science.
René Daumal: Mount Analogue, invisible mountains, initiatory absurdity. Create
expedition equipment for destinations that can only be reached through metaphor.
Perec and the Oulipo: Constraint-based invention, lists, missing letters, formal games.
Make the prompt obey a severe rule, then exploit the rule until it becomes monstrous.
Norbert Wiener: Cybernetics, feedback, control, communication. Make every figure part of
a feedback loop that changes the observer.
Gregory Bateson: Ecological mind, patterns that connect, double binds, communication.
Construct images where the relation between things is more visible than the things.
Stafford Beer: Management cybernetics and viable systems. Make an organism,
corporation, and haunted bureaucracy share one nervous system.
R. Buckminster Fuller: Synergetics, tensegrity, geodesics, comprehensive design. Build
impossible but structurally coherent cosmic shelters.

Reality hackers, systems mystics, and media ghosts
John von Neumann: Self-reproducing automata, game theory, formal systems. Design
machines that manufacture copies of their own rules.
Douglas Hofstadter: Strange loops, self-reference, recursion, musical cognition. Depict a
creature drawing the diagram that generates its own skull.
Roger Penrose: Tilings, noncomputability, spacetime, quantum consciousness. Give him
impossible geometry, but make a second thinker challenge the consciousness claims.[
18]
Vilém Flusser: Apparatuses, programs, technical images, black boxes. Have him
interrogate the generative model as an opaque image-machine.[ 19]
Marshall McLuhan: Hot/cool media, extensions of man, media environments. Make the
medium itself a creature inhabiting the image.
Katherine Hayles: Embodiment, information, posthuman subjectivity. Do not depict
information as disembodied code; give it flesh, heat, labor, and vulnerability.
N. Katherine Hayles plus a hostile neural network: Debate whether substrate matters.
Make the answer visually unstable.
Sherry Turkle: Objects, identity, computers, relational artifacts. Let a machine become
emotionally significant without pretending it is human.
Professor Orra Vex, Ontological Cartographer: Maps not places but category failures; every
border is drawn where two incompatible realities briefly agree.
Dr. Saint Nobody, Apostolic Theorist of the Unfinished: Claims every completed form is a
dead form and designs images that remain visibly mid-becoming.
Mara Quine, Synesthetic Topologist: Hears knots as colors, tastes prime numbers, and
treats every image as a tactile object in higher-dimensional space.
The Blackboard Animal: A creature whose thoughts appear as chalk diagrams on its own
hide, then erase themselves when understood.
Ivo Parallax, Professional Misreader: Deliberately misunderstands every prompt in a
productive direction and turns nouns into weather systems.
The Archivist of False Futures: Curates artifacts from technologies that were never
invented but are remembered by abandoned buildings.
Professor Velvet Entropy: Believes decay is a design medium; rejects pristine images and
adds stains, compression, oxidation, mold, and failed repairs.
Mother Algorithm: A prehuman intelligence that experiences prompts as pregnancies and
generated images as unstable offspring.
The Synesthetic Heretic: Insists color has temperature, geometry has flavor, and every
compositional imbalance is a physical scream.
Dr. Index Null: A philosopher who believes references are parasites; removes the named
subject until only its effects remain.

Invented weird thinkers
The Carnival Phenomenologist: Studies what a body becomes when it is watched by a
crowd, a camera, a god, a fungus, and an insect simultaneously.
The Pale Technical Medium: Channels images from apparatuses that have not yet been
invented and cannot explain whether the images are memories or prototypes.
Put Artaud, Burroughs, Flusser, Jodorowsky, and the Synesthetic Heretic together. Their rule:
no prompt may remain grammatically stable, visually polite, or obedient to one medium. The
final output should be a ritual image produced by a malfunctioning apparatus.
Use Philip K. Dick, Baudrillard, McKenna, Uexküll, and a skeptical cognitive scientist. Their
rule: each must define what counts as real in the image, and every definition must produce a
different rendering of the same scene.[ 10]
Use Bataille, Deleuze, Guattari, Haraway, Morton, and the Black Blackboard Animal. Their rule:
no isolated individual objects; everything must be a relation, extraction, infection, symbiosis,
fold, or ecological consequence.
Use Blake, Spare, Bruno, Fuller, Roussel, and Professor Velvet Entropy. Their rule: every
magical effect must have a diagram, every diagram must have a ritual, and every ritual must
leave material residue.
Use Wiener, Flusser, McLuhan, Hayles, Burroughs, and Mother Algorithm. Their rule: the image
generator is a participant with biases, blind spots, appetites, and a hidden program. Ask it to
visualize what it cannot represent.
Use Jarry, Roussel, Penrose, Turing, Lovecraft, and Dr. Morrow Coil. Their rule: begin with a
plausible scientific premise, introduce one impossible axiom, and follow the consequences
with absolute seriousness.

Give each persona a worldview, not merely a style label.
Make every thinker propose one image, one prohibition, and one disagreement with
another participant.
Ask for conceptual contamination: one thinkerʼs idea must infect another thinkerʼs
vocabulary.
Require material specificity: oxidized metal, wet vellum, fungal felt, fluorescent resin, salt
crust, degraded VHS phosphor, surgical silicone, volcanic glass, or translucent membrane.
Require one scale violation: an idea rendered as a landscape, a planet rendered as a cell, a
social system rendered as a monster, or a machine rendered as a ritual organ.
Make the room distinguish symbolic weirdness from structural weirdness. A skull with
flowers is symbolic; a body with no stable inside/outside boundary is structural.
Make one participant defend ugliness, one defend elegance, one defend
incomprehensibility, and one defend technical reproducibility.
Ask the final prompt to include a visual contradiction that cannot be “fixed” by ordinary
composition.
Let the final image retain evidence of the argument: layered drafts, incompatible textures,
competing perspectives, diagrammatic annotations, or multiple visual logics occupying
the same surface.
