# Cross-Media Operator Registry

This registry collapses repeated ideas from art, audio, Temporary Minds, semantic-system, and technical-roundtable sources into **functional operations**. It is deliberately smaller than the source corpus: new names belong here only when they change what a system can do, not when they merely rename a vibe.

Machine-readable companion: [`../../machine/operators.json`](../../machine/operators.json).

## How to read an entry

- **PROCEDURAL** means the operation is explicitly imposed by a prompt/controller/software. It does not claim to alter native model internals.
- **HYPOTHESIS** means the observable recipe may be useful, but the causal explanation still needs controls/ablation.
- **SPECULATIVE** means the project has a plausible experiment but little evidence yet.
- **OBSERVED PATTERN** means a family of behavior has been seen in iterative practice; it remains model/version dependent.

# A. Ontology, identity, and category

### Property Unbundling / Ownership Transfer
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Detach a property normally owned by A, assign it to B, remove it from A, then follow the consequences of the new ownership literally.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #1.

### Error Axiomatization / Shadow Taxonomy
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Find a coherent near-misclassification, reject random wrongness, construct the minimum world where the misclassification is literally correct, then reason rigorously inside that taxonomy.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #5.

### Primitive Deletion / Constitutive Vacuum
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Delete one load-bearing primitive **without replacement**. Audit what depended on it and reconstruct around the wound rather than sneaking in a synonym.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #6.

### Ontological Decompilation + Cascading Recompilation
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Replace a foundational primitive with a different one, identify genuine dependencies, and rederive downstream concepts from the new base.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #11.

**Do not collapse this with Primitive Deletion:** deletion leaves a constitutive absence; recompilation installs a replacement.

### Literal Identity Collapse / Oneiric Contamination
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Assert an incompatible identity A=B as literal rather than metaphorical; minimally revise surrounding assumptions when consequences force a repair.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #13.

### Ontological Flipping / Kinetic Dissolution
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Make the maintained process primary and the visible object secondary. Rewrite nouns as rates, flows, gradients, couplings, boundaries, maintenance burdens, and decay processes.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #8; image practice calls the related move **Ontological Flipping**.

### Systemic Taxonomic Contagion
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Import the **operational lifecycle or rule system** of a foreign taxonomy into host functions. Import causality, not mushrooms/courts/germs/other costume from the source domain.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #12.

# B. Distance, analogy, and cross-domain transport

### Alien Distance Metric
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Change the ruler defining conceptual nearness: failure behavior, maintenance burden, residue, dependency position, collapse trajectory, observer distortion, etc. Choose neighbors because they are close under the new ruler.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #15.

### Destructive Compression / Alien Substrate Transference
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Strip a problem to a causal invariant, destroy its surface/domain identity, reinstantiate the invariant in an unrelated substrate, solve there using native rules, then bring back only structural consequences.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #4.

### Cross-Domain Simulation / Concept Transduction
**Status:** PROCEDURAL; mechanism claims may be HYPOTHESIS  
Translate actual source-domain behavior into target-medium affordances and controls: reaction–diffusion into motif growth/inhibition, phase interference into reinforcement/cancellation, topology into anatomy continuity, thermodynamics into threshold/state change.

Rule: **borrow dynamics, not vocabulary**.

# C. Attention, perception, salience, and selection

### Attentional Shadow Harvesting
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Interrupt exhaustive attention to the obvious center, enumerate what the focus made easy to ignore, promote one structurally legitimate neglected node, and use it before explanation domesticates it.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #3.

### Selective Cognitive Lesion / Primitive Ablation
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Remove one narrow cognitive operation for the task and force compensatory procedures while other reasoning remains normal.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #16.

### Synthetic Transducer
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Invent a detector for an abstract property, define a synthetic sensation/measurement for it, then let that signal alter downstream salience or action.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #17.

### Synthetic Valence
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Attach an invented attraction, aversion, tenderness, protection, or other weighting to a **detectable** trigger; let it alter attention, preservation, and selection.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #18.

### Alien Utility Function / Generative Desire
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Keep user requirements as hard constraints, but rank otherwise-valid candidates using an explicit nonstandard objective.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #21.

### Observational Crystallization / Attentional Collapse
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Treat deliberate inspection as a commitment operation: attended dimensions become fixed/committed while unexamined dimensions retain freedom to mutate.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #24.

### Minority-Axis Sovereignty
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Run incompatible readings separately, crown the most anomalous legitimate reading, and make the others resist/constrain it rather than averaging everything into consensus.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #14.

# D. Constraint conflict and negative-space methods

### Separated Jurisdictions
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Give independent subsystems different owners/rules rather than blending. Audio example: harmony, melody, rhythm, timbre, performance, and anchor each obey different systems.

Source: Productive Contradiction Under Constraint / Suno sources.

### Anchor + Mutation Field
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Hold one recognizable element stable while surrounding dimensions deform. Examples: identity anchor in images, invariant pulse/tone in audio, persistent symbol mapping in a controller.

### Negative Load-Bearing Synthesis
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Negate a hidden assumption while preserving the goal, then require the negation itself to become the means by which the goal remains achievable.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #2.

### Procedural Taboo + Aftermath
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Ban the obvious mechanism and an easy workaround. Pursue another independently justified process; accept the target only if it appears as collateral aftermath rather than direct construction.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #20.

### Omission Field / Negative Semantic Triangulation
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Keep target X real but prohibit direct representation. Triangulate it from multiple independent external consequences and generate from their intersection.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #9.

# E. Time, memory, causality, and path dependence

### Teleological Bootstrap Inversion
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Fix a terminal condition first, then reconstruct the present/ancestry so the terminal state becomes causally inevitable under the new system.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #7.

### Recall Mutation
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Every recall happens under a context that scars the remembered concept. The scarred descendant becomes the next remembered version.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #23.

### Semantic Recoil
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
A later state changes the active interpretation of an earlier state; subsequent reasoning uses A′ rather than restoring pristine A. In strong mode, the interpreter rule itself may mutate.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #25; also central to Semantic Manifold route design.

### Descendant Fitness
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Breed candidate ideas forward and choose the present seed by the richness/fertility of its descendants rather than by current attractiveness.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #22.

### Hysteretic / Non-Commutative Iteration
**Status:** HYPOTHESIS for native generator dynamics; PROCEDURAL for external controllers  
Apply transformations in ordered loops and compare `A→B→A`, `B→A→B`, and direct endpoints. Test whether order and history produce irreversible residue.

Related: DAVID NCHR, iterative image/remix chains, Semantic Manifold path memory.

### Cybernetic Circuit Closure + Deutero-Learning
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Model the feedback relation that reproduces a problem, close the loop explicitly, then alter the higher-order premise that keeps recreating the same loop.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #10.

### Forced Aliasing / Lossy Cognition
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Deliberately destroy representational dimensions until distant concepts collide, then reconstruct without restoring the information that made them distinguishable.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #19.

### Token / Capability Tax
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Systematically remove or mutate allowed expressive units across externally tracked cycles. Useful in SRE experiments; do not describe it as literal neural capability loss.

### Symbolic Compression
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Replace recurring structures with glyphs/symbols whose roles, inheritance, and mappings are explicitly stored and can participate in later rules.

# G. Evolutionary and meta-generative operators

### Meta-Genomic Speciation / Ecosystemic Axiom Breeding
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Treat cognitive rules themselves as a breeding population. Cross/mutate rules, require offspring to be irreducible to either parent, and let successful outputs alter the next fitness landscape.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #26.

### Structural Satiation / Conceptual Allergy
**Status:** PROCEDURAL REGULATOR  
Fingerprint the structural skeleton of a successful move and temporarily reject near-repeat solutions even when the surface vocabulary changes.

Source: Temporary Minds support architecture.

### Cliché Mortality / Operator Burnout
**Status:** PROCEDURAL REGULATOR  
Apply temporary cost or retirement to successful operators so one productive trick does not become the project’s new generic default.

# H. Material, medium, and capture coupling

### Material Anchoring
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Translate abstract geometry/relations into concrete fabrication or material behavior so the generator has physical consequences to satisfy: foam latex, silicone, emulsion, magnetic tape, ice, membrane, resonant metal, etc.

### Causal Artifact Chain
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Do not merely request “VHS/glitch.” Specify a capture/transfer/degradation process whose failures cause artifacts: telecine generation loss, head switching, chroma bleed, tracking drag, phase cancellation, dropped-frame timing, codec feedback.

### Observer / Medium Coupling
**Status:** SPECULATIVE/PROCEDURAL  
Make the observation or recording apparatus subject to the same rule affecting the represented object, or make observer action create a documented external perturbation.

# I. Feedback, attractors, and fossilization

### Recursive Artifact Fossilization
**Status:** OBSERVED PATTERN / PROCEDURAL RECIPE  
Take a generated accident, encode/downsample/remix/recondition it, and use it as next-pass input so repair noise or an error can become stable structure.

### Attractor Lock → Perturb
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Let a recurring regime stabilize long enough to characterize it, perturb one controlled variable, then observe absorption, escape, or reorganization.

Used by SRE/TOPOS and the broader experimental method.

# Graduation rule

A phrase belongs in this registry when its operation can be stated **without the aesthetic costume** and it changes what transitions, outputs, or selections become legal/likely.

For technical mechanism claims, also require a plausible control and falsification path. A cool artifact can stay cool even if its causal story gets demoted.
