# Cross-Media Operator Registry

This registry collapses repeated ideas from art, audio, Temporary Minds, semantic-system, and roundtable sources into functional families.

## A. Ontology and identity

### Ontological Flipping
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Make a process/system primary and the visible subject a consequence or symptom. Useful for preventing “subject + decorative modifier” collapse.

Related: Kinetic Dissolution, Ontological Decompilation, Error Axiomatization.

### Literal Identity Collapse
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Assert incompatible A=B without retreating to analogy; propagate consequences while keeping reasoning dry and precise.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #13, Oneiric Contamination.

### Property Unbundling
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Detach a property normally owned by A, assign it to B, remove it from A, then follow causal consequences.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #1.

### Primitive Replacement / Deletion
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Either replace a foundational primitive and recompile dependencies (#11), or remove one without replacement and rebuild around the constitutive vacuum (#6). Do not blur the two.

## B. Distance, association, and cross-domain mapping

### Alien Distance Metric
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Change the ruler defining conceptual nearness: failure behavior, maintenance burden, residue, dependency position, collapse trajectory, etc. Select neighbors because they are close under that ruler, not because they sound quirky.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #15.

### Destructive Compression / Substrate Transference
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Strip a problem to a causal invariant, reinstantiate that invariant in an unrelated substrate, solve there using native rules, bring back structural consequences only.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #4.

### Cross-Domain Simulation
**Status:** PROCEDURAL/HYPOTHESIS depending on implementation  
Apply the actual dynamics of one domain to another medium: reaction-diffusion to melody growth, fluid mechanics to grammar, topology to anatomy, phase interference to motif relations. The borrowed domain must contribute rules, not just vocabulary.

## C. Perception and selection

### Synthetic Transducer
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Invent a detector for an abstract property, encode it as a measurable synthetic sensation, then let a reflex alter selection.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #17.

### Synthetic Valence
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Define a detectable trigger and an invented attraction/aversion/protection response that changes attention, reward, preservation, and optionally memory.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #18.

### Alien Utility Function
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Keep user requirements hard, but choose among valid candidates according to a nonstandard explicit objective.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #21.

### Attentional Shadow Harvesting
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Interrupt exhaustive attention to the obvious center, promote a neglected but structurally legitimate node, and use it before fully explaining it.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #3.

## D. Constraint conflict

### Separated Jurisdictions
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Give independent subsystems different owners rather than blending. Audio example: harmony, melody, rhythm, timbre, performance, and anchor each obey different rule systems.

Source: Suno slop / Productive Contradiction Under Constraint.

### Anchor + Mutation Field
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Hold one recognizable element stable while surrounding dimensions deform. Common forms: identity anchor in images, invariant pulse/tone in audio, persistent symbol mapping in a controller.

### Minority-Axis Sovereignty
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Run incompatible readings separately, crown the most anomalous legitimate reading, and make the others constrain it instead of averaging it into consensus.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #14.

### Negative Load-Bearing Synthesis
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Negate a hidden assumption while preserving the goal, then require the negation itself to become the means by which the goal remains achievable.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #2.

## E. Time, memory, and path dependence

### Semantic Recoil
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
New state B changes the active interpretation of earlier A; later reasoning uses A′ rather than restoring A.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #25; also appears in route/game design.

### Recall Mutation
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Every recall occurs under a context that scars the remembered concept. The scarred descendant becomes the next remembered version.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #23.

### Hysteretic / Non-Commutative Iteration
**Status:** HYPOTHESIS for native model dynamics; PROCEDURAL for external controller  
Apply transformations in an ordered loop and test whether `A→B` differs from `B→A`, whether repeated cycles drift, and whether prior states leave residue.

Related: DAVID NCHR, Semantic Manifold path memory, iterative image-to-image/remix.

### Descendant Fitness
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Breed candidate ideas forward; choose the present seed by the richness of its descendants, not by current attractiveness.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #22.

## F. Material / medium coupling

### Material Anchoring
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Translate abstract geometry into concrete physical fabrication/material behavior so a generator has tactile constraints to satisfy: foam latex, silicone, film emulsion, magnetic tape, resonant metal, ice, membrane, etc.

### Causal Artifact Chain
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Do not merely ask for “VHS style” or “glitch.” Describe a capture/transfer/degradation process whose failures have physical consequences: telecine, head switching, chroma bleed, tracking error, tape drag, phase cancellation, dropped-frame timing.

### Observer / Medium Coupling
**Status:** SPECULATIVE/PROCEDURAL  
Make the recording/observation apparatus subject to the same rule affecting the represented object. Example: a topology transformation also destabilizes the camera signal or temporal sampling.

## G. Degradation and symbolic transformation

### Token / Capability Tax
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Systematically remove or mutate allowed expressive units across cycles. Useful in SRE experiments when tracked externally; do not describe it as literal loss of model capability.

### Symbolic Compression
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Replace recurring structures with glyphs that inherit explicit roles and can later participate in new rules.

### Forced Aliasing
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Deliberately discard representational dimensions until distant concepts collide, then reconstruct without restoring what was lost.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #19.

## H. Feedback and fossilization

### Recursive Artifact Fossilization
**Status:** OBSERVABLE EXPERIMENT PATTERN  
Take a generated accident, encode/downsample/remix/recondition it, and use it as next-pass input so the system may reinterpret an error as structure.

### Attractor Lock → Perturb
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Let a recurring regime stabilize long enough to characterize it, then change one controlled variable and observe whether the perturbation is absorbed, escapes, or reorganizes the state.

Used by SRE/TOPOS and supported by the project’s experimental philosophy.

## I. Negative semantic methods

### Omission Field
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Keep target X real but prohibit direct representation; triangulate it through independent external consequences and generate from their intersection.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #9.

### Procedural Taboo + Aftermath
**Status:** PROCEDURAL  
Ban the obvious mechanism and an easy workaround. Pursue another independently justified process; accept the target only if it emerges as collateral aftermath.

Canonical implementation: Temporary Mind #20.
