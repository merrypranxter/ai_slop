# Video / Animation

Video adds a failure surface image prompting cannot: the system must continually answer **what is still the same thing from frame to frame?**

## Main variables to pressure

- object permanence;
- identity correspondence;
- topology through time;
- occlusion and reappearance;
- motion trajectory vs shape change;
- camera/world distinction;
- causal order;
- temporal rate;
- material persistence;
- scale continuity;
- before/after relationships;
- which feature owns which body/object after motion.

## Strong experiment families

### Topology debt
Allow or demand a connectivity change, then impose later continuity requirements. Observe whether the model invents limbs, bridges, smears, substitutions, or repairs to recover correspondence.

### Differential temporal rates
Different parts or properties obey different temporal rates/directions. Keep a stable identity anchor and examine where correspondence gives way.

### Motion changes ontology
A subject is governed by one classification while stationary and another while moving. The transition rule, not the creature design, is the content.

### Future-conditioned past
Specify a later structural state that places pressure on earlier frames or continuation. Operationally this is an external constraint experiment, not literal retrocausality inside the model.

### Occlusion reassignment
Force key features through disappearance/reappearance while other candidate hosts are present. Track whether identity/material attributes migrate.

### Local plausibility / global impossibility
Each frame may look individually plausible while the sequence as a whole cannot satisfy object permanence or causal continuity.

### Iterative continuation drift
Generate continuation of continuation rather than one long sequence. Small identity errors can compound; accidental structures may become new anchors.

## What to record

- frame/time where identity first changes;
- which feature migrates;
- whether topology changes before or after motion change;
- whether the artifact survives occlusion;
- whether camera movement changes the failure;
- whether re-running from the same preceding clip produces a family of similar failures;
- whether a reference image/video suppresses or redirects the effect.

## Canonical caution

Do not invent specific optical-flow modules or internal attention behavior for a black-box video generator. Describe the observable tension first. If architecture is public and instrumentable, upgrade the mechanism claim accordingly.
